TESTING SERVICES

Four ways GeneMatch is used, all built on the same laboratory-first process.

Each service below describes a use case for the same underlying platform. In every case, the genetic profile comes from an accredited laboratory. GeneMatch handles the case management, comparison, and reporting around it.

DNA testing / Relationship testing / Parentage testing / Profile comparison

DNA testing

DNA testing is the laboratory step: extracting genetic material from a sample and reading it with validated testing, genotyping, or sequencing equipment. GeneMatch does not perform this step. We coordinate the request, track the sample, and receive the resulting profile from a partner laboratory.

If you're looking to start a test, the request a test page walks through sample types, consent requirements, and what happens after collection.

Relationship testing

Beyond parent and child, GeneMatch supports comparison against sibling, half-sibling, grandparent-grandchild, and avuncular hypotheses. The analysis engine compares competing hypotheses rather than testing a single assumed relationship in isolation, which matters when the people involved aren't sure which relationship they're actually checking.

Every relationship report states the method used, the reference dataset, and the analyst who reviewed it.

Parentage testing

Parentage cases compare a child's profile against one or both alleged parents. GeneMatch's statistical engine supports exclusion, inclusion and compatibility analysis, and likelihood ratio calculations, using population allele frequency data configured by the laboratory's scientific team.

A parentage report from GeneMatch is a software-generated document based on laboratory data. Whether it carries legal weight depends on the jurisdiction and whether the case followed a legally recognized chain-of-custody and testing process; GeneMatch surfaces a configurable flag for this rather than deciding it automatically.

Genetic profile comparison

At the core of every service above is the same comparison engine: two or more genetic profiles, checked marker by marker, with matching markers, non-matching markers, missing data, and quality warnings all reported separately rather than collapsed into a single "match percentage."

A percentage-style score is only shown where it has a scientifically valid interpretation for the specific test performed. Otherwise, results are reported as likelihood ratios or probabilities with their assumptions stated alongside them.
